Why artists choose a smaller room

Intimate by design

Large commercial galleries can feel distant. Here, visitors stand close to the work, openings feel like gatherings of neighbours, and artists are part of a building full of other makers — studios down the hall, conversation in the corridor, and a community that shows up for one another.

“A room where the work — and the people who made it — come first.”

About

A gallery rooted in its building — and its neighbourhood.

Our gallery is the public face of an artists’ community building in Montreal: a place where studios, workshops, and working artists share one address, and where the exhibition space opens that life to the wider city.

At about 1,000 square feet, the gallery is deliberately small. That scale is the point. Exhibitions here are close-up and unhurried; openings are conversations rather than crowds; and artists — especially those mounting a first or second show — get a professional room without the pressure and distance of a large commercial gallery.

We welcome painters, photographers, textile artists, sculptors, printmakers, and interdisciplinary artists, as well as collectives and community groups with a story to tell on a wall.

Community first

The gallery exists for the artists in the building and the neighbours around it. Programming favours local work and genuine connection over spectacle.

Artist-run spirit

Decisions are made by people who make work themselves. Artists keep a strong voice in how their exhibition looks, reads, and opens.

Montreal, in both languages

We work in a bilingual city and a bilingual building. Exhibitions and events reflect the mix of cultures that makes Montreal’s art scene what it is.
